Most arguments about remote work miss the point. The question isn't whether people are more productive at home or in an office; the honest answer is that it depends on the person, the task, and the week. The real question is who gets to decide.

For most of the last century, the answer was the employer, and the office was the default because it made supervision easy. The pandemic showed that a lot of work could be done without that supervision, and that many managers had been mistaking presence for output. Some companies took that lesson seriously. Others spent 2023 and 2024 walking it back, usually with language about culture and collaboration that was hard to distinguish from a preference for control.

I don't think fully remote is right for everyone, and I've seen new hires struggle without the informal help that comes from sitting near experienced colleagues. But a policy that treats adults as suspects until proven otherwise costs more than it saves. The companies that will keep the best people are the ones that set clear expectations for results and then let people meet them however works.

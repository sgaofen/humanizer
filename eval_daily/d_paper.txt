Our experiments show that the proposed method improves classification accuracy on all three benchmarks. On CIFAR-100 it reaches 94.2%, a gain of 3.7 percentage points over the strongest prior method reported by Zhang et al. (2021). The improvement is largest in the low-data setting: with only 500 labeled examples, our approach outperforms the baselines by 8.1 points, and the margin narrows steadily as more labels become available. Ablations indicate that roughly 60% of the overall gain comes from the contrastive pretraining stage, with the remainder attributable to the modified attention mechanism described in Section 3.2. Removing either component alone degrades performance, but removing both drops accuracy below the baseline, which suggests the two interact rather than contribute independently. We also observe that training remains stable across the five random seeds we tested, with a standard deviation of 0.3 points on CIFAR-100. Section 5 discusses the computational cost of the pretraining stage, which is the main practical limitation of the method.

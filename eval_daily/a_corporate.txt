Here's a summary of where things stand after the Q3 review. Customer acquisition cost rose 23% quarter over quarter, driven mostly by heavier competition in paid search, where CPCs climbed across our core keyword set. At the same time, retention improved from 82% to 87%, which suggests the product investments we made in the spring are starting to pay off. These two trends aren't contradictory. Taken together, they point to a shift toward a smaller number of higher-value customers who cost more to acquire but stay longer. Based on this, leadership recommends moving 15% of the Q4 acquisition budget into retention programs, with a particular focus on onboarding and the first 30 days. We also recommend building a more granular attribution model so that channel-level decisions in Q1 rest on better data than they do today. I'd suggest we revisit both decisions at the January review once we have a full quarter of retention data under the new allocation.

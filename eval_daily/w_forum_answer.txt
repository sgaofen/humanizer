The short answer is that your sourdough isn't rising because your starter isn't ready, not because of the flour.

A starter that doubles in 4 to 6 hours at room temperature is ready to use. If yours takes 12 hours or more, it will still leaven bread, but slowly, and the dough will overproof on the surface while staying dense underneath, which matches what you describe. The fix is to feed it twice a day for three or four days at a 1:1:1 ratio and keep it somewhere around 75 degrees. A turned-off oven with the light on works.

Two other things worth checking. First, bread flour with at least 12% protein will give you a noticeably better rise than all-purpose. Second, if your kitchen is cold, extend the bulk fermentation rather than adding more starter; more starter just makes the dough sour and doesn't fix the timing.

Once the starter is reliable, most of the other variables stop mattering as much as people online suggest.
